## Coverage

The item is IOM's monthly Flow Monitoring Registry (FMR) dashboard/
infographic for Yemen, covering **August 2026**. It reports counts of
migrants entering and leaving Yemen through IOM's Displacement Tracking
Matrix (DTM) flow-monitoring points in several Yemeni governorates
(Ta'iz, Lahj, Abyan, Shabwah, Al Maharah), plus indicative estimates of
irregular migration flows, their origin/destination and drivers. Countries
tagged: Yemen, Djibouti, Ethiopia, Oman, Somalia. ReliefWeb classifies the
item under "Protection and Human Rights" and lists its format as
**Infographic** — the RSS `<description>` nonetheless carried a
substantive narrative body (not just "refer to attached"), which is why it
was picked.

## Findings (own wording)

IOM's Yemen DTM recorded roughly 11,400 migrants entering the country in
August 2026, a drop of about one-sixth from the July 2026 count (roughly
13,700). Most of that inbound flow crossed from Djibouti — the release
attributes about three in five arrivals to that route — with the rest
split between Somalia and, in a smaller share, Oman. Arrivals from
Djibouti landed mostly in Abyan governorate, with the remainder entering
via Ta'iz; migrants coming from Somalia were tracked entirely through
Shabwah, and a few hundred people crossed from Oman into Al Maharah's
Shahin district. The inbound population skewed heavily male (roughly
three in five), with women and children each making up smaller shares
(children being the smallest group, under a fifth).

Outbound movement was much smaller in volume — under 1,700 people tracked
leaving Yemen in the month — and went almost entirely toward Djibouti
(via Lahj governorate), with a handful of people departing toward Oman
from Al Maharah.

## Publishing organization's own claims vs. interpretation

The specific counts, percentages, and governorate breakdowns above are all
IOM's own DTM-derived figures as stated in the release; they have not been
independently verified here against another source. The only contribution
added in this report is the paraphrase/restructuring of that data (e.g.
describing the month-over-month change, grouping origin/destination
points) and the observation that the inbound flow substantially outweighs
the outbound flow in this reporting period — IOM's release does not itself
frame the data that way, it simply lists the figures.
