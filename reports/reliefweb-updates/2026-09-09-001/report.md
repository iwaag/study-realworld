## What happened

Following earthquakes in June 2026 that damaged water and sanitation
infrastructure in Venezuela's La Guaira state, UNICEF and Venezuela's
Ministry of People's Power for Water Services are preparing to rehabilitate
two facilities: the Mamo pumping station and the Naiguatá water treatment
plant. Work is planned to start in October 2026.

## Scale and funding

Combined, the two projects are expected to benefit roughly 250,000 people,
including about 76,200 children (Mamo: ~150,000 people / 45,700 children;
Naiguatá: ~100,000 people / 30,500 children). Since the earthquakes,
UNICEF-supported emergency measures have already given 16,661 people
(5,079 children) access to safe water, but the release says heavy rains and
rising operational costs are straining these temporary arrangements —
the stated reason for moving to permanent repairs (filtration/chlorination
systems, pumping equipment).

Of the US$65.7 million UNICEF says the overall earthquake response
requires, US$25.7 million has been mobilized, leaving a roughly US$40
million gap; the WASH (water, sanitation and hygiene) component specifically
is reported to be about 24% funded.

## Attribution

UNICEF Venezuela Representative Manuel Rodríguez Pumarol is quoted
describing the move to durable water services as the "critical next step"
in recovery, and the release appeals for continued partner support.
