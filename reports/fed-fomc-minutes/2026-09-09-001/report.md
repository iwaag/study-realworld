# FOMC Minutes, July 28–29, 2026 — Summary

Source: Federal Reserve, "Minutes of the Federal Open Market Committee,
July 28–29, 2026,"
https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/files/fomcminutes20260729.pdf

## Policy decision

The Committee voted 9–3 to hold the federal funds rate target range at
3½–3¾ percent, continuing its ample-reserves operating framework. Beth
Hammack, Neel Kashkari, and Lorie Logan dissented, each preferring a
quarter-point increase. The Board separately voted unanimously to keep the
interest rate on reserve balances at 3.65 percent and the primary credit
rate at 3.75 percent. The post-meeting statement repeated June's language
that the Committee "will deliver price stability," language the minutes
note "almost all members" agreed to retain.

## Economic picture

Inflation was still running well above target: total PCE inflation was 4.1
percent in May before staff estimated it stepped down to about 3.7 percent
in June, with core PCE easing to roughly 3.3 percent. Staff and
participants attributed the elevated readings to a mix of lingering tariff
effects, higher energy and input costs tied to an escalating conflict in
the Middle East, and demand pressure from the ongoing AI-related
infrastructure buildout (materials such as chips and steel, plus consumer
items like smartphones and electricity, were cited as showing notable price
increases). The unemployment rate held at 4.2 percent, job gains had
broadened beyond healthcare and social assistance, and wage growth remained
moderate. Growth had slowed somewhat in GDP terms but underlying private
demand (consumption plus fixed investment) was described as picking up,
supported heavily by AI-related business investment.

## Discussion themes

Participants were split on how persistent the inflation overshoot would
prove: most expected further step-down as tariff and energy effects fade,
but many flagged the risk that years of above-target inflation could start
shifting expectations and wage/price-setting behavior. Financial-stability
discussion centered on the financing of the AI infrastructure buildout —
elevated equity valuations for AI-linked firms, growing reliance on
borrowing (including from nonbank lenders and regional banks) to fund
capital spending, and hedge-fund leverage near record highs — alongside a
noted incident involving a disruption to transaction settlements that the
ample-reserves regime helped absorb without disorder. The Committee also
discussed, without deciding, a possible move to six scheduled meetings per
year (from eight) and continued to defer substantive balance-sheet-policy
choices to a dedicated task force review.
