# Refugee Admissions to the US Are Dropping Sharply in Trump's Second Term

## Coverage

The document is a short-read article covering U.S. refugee admissions trends
from fiscal year 2001 through fiscal year 2026 (through July 31, 2026),
including a breakdown by country of origin for FY2026 admissions, an
international comparison of refugee resettlement volumes for calendar year
2025 (U.S. vs. Canada and Australia), and policy context around the Trump
administration's 2025 executive order on Afrikaner resettlement priority.
It does not cover asylum seekers, internal displacement, or non-U.S.
migration policy in depth - its scope is specifically the formal U.S.
refugee admissions/resettlement program.

## Methodology (as reported by Pew)

Pew reports that the analysis draws on U.S. Department of State Refugee
Processing Center admissions data and UNHCR Refugee Data Finder data for
international comparisons. The article does not describe an original Pew
survey; it is a data-journalism piece built on administrative and
international-organization statistics rather than Pew's own polling.

## Key findings

Pew reports that refugee admissions to the U.S. have fallen sharply in
fiscal year 2026: only 10,258 refugees were admitted through July 31, 2026,
compared with 38,102 in all of FY2025 and 100,034 in FY2024. Pew reports
that the FY2026 admissions ceiling of 17,500 is the lowest the U.S.
government has set since at least the turn of the century.

Pew reports a striking compositional shift: of the 10,258 refugees admitted
in FY2026, 10,255 (essentially all of them) came from South Africa, while
only 3 came from Afghanistan and effectively none from the Democratic
Republic of Congo - countries that were previously among the largest source
countries for U.S.-bound refugees. Pew attributes this shift to a February
2025 executive order that directed the government to prioritize resettling
Afrikaners, citing claims of "government-sponsored race-based
discrimination" against them in South Africa; Pew notes these claims "have
been disputed."

Pew also reports an international comparison for calendar year 2025: Canada
resettled roughly 39,000 refugees (the global leader), Australia around
19,000, and the U.S. approximately 11,500 - meaning the U.S., historically
the world's largest refugee resettlement country, now trails both Canada and
Australia in absolute numbers.

This suggests that the drop in U.S. refugee admissions is not simply a
matter of reduced overall capacity but reflects a deliberate reallocation of
a shrunken program toward a single, politically chosen origin group, at the
expense of the broader set of refugee populations the U.S. program has
historically served. It also suggests a shift in the U.S.'s relative
standing among major resettlement countries, from clear leader to a
secondary position behind Canada and Australia.

## Takeaway

The report's central point is a sharp, policy-driven contraction and
redirection of the U.S. refugee program in FY2026: total admissions are at a
multi-decade low, and the admissions that do occur are now overwhelmingly
concentrated on Afrikaners from South Africa rather than the more diverse
mix of refugee-origin countries (e.g., Afghanistan, DRC) seen in recent
prior years.
